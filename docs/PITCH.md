# RESPAWN — 3-minute pitch

**Hook (20 s).** "Raise your hand if you've already made a decision this week that will matter in 2100." Nobody does. "You did. Which job you'll take, how you'll travel, what you'll do with your money: those choices add up. RESPAWN lets you live a whole life in 12 minutes, and then shows you the bill."

**What it is (40 s).** A pixel-art life sim on your phone. You start your first week at AIDAMS: PM's 8AM class, the sète, the exchange, your first internship. Then life: the offer from a defense-analytics giant or the climate startup, grinding 55-hour weeks or a four-day week, kids, a startup that pays out, a yacht or your grandparents' retirement home. About twenty big choices, a character sheet that grows with you (Intelligence, Math & AI, Economics, Charisma…), and every scene written for *you* by Claude.

**The twist (40 s).** When you die, you get three things. Your life radar. **The Earth in 2100 if 8 billion people lived like you**: temperature, sea level, insects, people in extreme heat. And your **carbon receipt**, line by line, every number sourced from the IEA, ADEME, the IPCC. Then the arcade screen: **RESPAWN?** Go back to the choice that weighed most, take the other path, and watch both Earths side by side.

**Why it lands (30 s).** No lectures. The message comes from trade-offs. And the data teaches things people don't expect: your flights and your job outweigh your screen time; your phone's footprint is mostly made before you open the box; AI can push emissions up or cut them, depending on what you build with it. Even the most frugal life stays above 2 °C, because system change matters: the jobs you take matter.

**How it's built (30 s).** Data-driven: all the content lives in JSON files anyone in the team can edit. A deterministic engine decides every number; Claude only writes the words, so the AI can never break the science. Pixel art from our own logo, deployed on GitHub Pages, open source.

**Close (20 s).** "Scan the QR code, live your life, and tell us your temperature. One life. Twenty choices. Eight billion consequences."
